The Field Notes Dispatch

July 2026

Issue #2 · Monthly newsletter on cycle tourism in British Columbia

It's the heart of BC riding season, and this issue is a full one.

May and June took us on the road. At the Canadian Trail Summit in Winnipeg, I (Heather Piwowar) presented on cycle tourism in the tourism breakout. A few weeks earlier, the Outdoor Recreation Council of BC's 50th anniversary conference in Kamloops had me and Colleen MacDonald on a panel about sustainable tourism. Two rooms, one message: trails are economic infrastructure, and BC's are worth investing in.

Closer to home, the BC Cycle Tourism Society just had its first board meeting. A small step on paper, a big one for us.

In this issue: 145 places across BC have earned their place on BC Bike Stops, we're looking at the law behind every road you ride, and we've rounded up the latest cycling news, trip reports, and ways to get involved.

The Feature

145 places in BC that want you to visit with your bike#

July 30, 2026

We hit a milestone this month: the first 145 accredited places went live on BC Bike Stops. These aren't paid placements or self-nominations we took on faith. Each one earned it the same way: by showing us, in their own words, how they welcome cyclists.

Red Beard Cafe in Kamloops was one of the first to be accredited. "Red Beard is just off the Rivers Trail on the flat North Shore of Kamloops. We have many bike racks, and locks if you forget yours." Locks if you forget yours. That's exactly the kind of detail we were hoping to find.

In Vancouver, Our Community Bikes has been fixing bikes and teaching people to fix their own since long before we showed up. "We strive to create an environment where all people feel safe, included and respected."

And Accent Inns has made bike-friendliness a chain-wide promise across five BC locations. "Love your bike? Bring it to your room." There's also a wash station and a 37-piece tool kit.

It's not just cafes, shops, and hotels. At The Butchart Gardens, "it is always a pleasure for us to welcome bikers." And Kelowna's Okanagan Heritage Museum encourages visitors to arrive by bike.

The full directory now covers more than 18,000 places across BC, from water refills to welcoming beds. New to BC Bike Stops? We introduced the program in our launch announcement. The accredited ones are where someone has gone further and put it in writing, on their website or to us directly. Know a business that belongs on the list? Send them to bccycletourism.ca/apply.

— Heather

News from the field

July 13–15

Sea to Sky corridor stays in public hands

Transportation Minister Mike Farnworth confirmed the province will keep the former BC Rail corridor, Squamish to Prince George, in public hands. Two days later we met with MLA Jeremy Valeriote to talk about how cycling could fit into the corridor's future. Ministerial statement →

July

A bikepacking gear library opens in Nelson

Building Momentum Bikepacking, a Nelson group creating space for women, non-binary and gender non-conforming riders, launched a free gear library: bikes, bags, and camping gear on 7 to 14-day loans, donations welcome, and borrowing open to everyone. An Okanagan group is already asking how to copy the model. Gear library →

July

Two ways to speak up for backcountry access

The Outdoor Recreation Council of BC wants to know which resource roads matter for your riding, and has a new guide for weighing in on the forest landscape plans now underway around the province. Resource road survey → and Forest planning guide →

July 3–17

New 14 km on the Shuswap North Okanagan Rail Trail

A new 14 km section between Rosemond Lake Bridge and Grindrod is open, celebrated with a July 17 ribbon cutting at Mara Trailhead alongside development partner Splatsin te Secwépemc. About 32 km of the Sicamous to Armstrong corridor is now rideable, across three separate segments. Castanet →

June 18

Kettle Valley Rail Trail: the door reopens a crack

The province stepped partway back from decommissioning the damaged Kettle Valley Rail Trail sections near Princeton. Repairs to three priority segments are now on the table, pending federal disaster-assistance funding and a long-term maintenance commitment from the regional district, whose board decides by the end of 2026. The hardest-hit 17 km near Coquihalla Lakes is still being decommissioned this summer, though the railbed stays, and the province says it is open to reroute proposals from Trans Canada Trail. Progress, not a rescue. Province's announcement →
