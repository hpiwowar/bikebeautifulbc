The Feature

Ride BC indoors this winter

November 27, 2026

Riding the Stanley Park loop on Rouvy · Screenshot: Rouvy

In the past year I've pedalled more than 90 hours indoors, keeping up my exercise habit when the cold or wet have made it otherwise unappealing to ride outside. Luckily, I've spent very little of that time looking at the wall: I was riding around Stanley Park, down the Okanagan Rail Trail, and through the Rockies to Emerald Lake.

It isn't the same as being there, but it has been a fun way to get a sneak peek of bits of BC I haven't been to yet.

The free way: YouTube

All you need is a phone or tablet propped on the handlebars, or a TV in front of the bike. Go to YouTube, find a ride someone filmed from the saddle, press play, and pedal along. It works on any stationary bike, including the spin bikes at the gym.

Three BC rides to start with:

  • The Myra Canyon trestles above Kelowna, 37 minutes, from Hiking British Columbia.
  • Around Stanley Park on the seawall, 37 minutes, from Vancouver POV.
  • The Westside Legacy Trail from Invermere to Fairmont Hot Springs, 24 minutes, from Don Barnett.

We've mapped 432 more rider's-view YouTube rides around BC. They're all on our BC rides on YouTube page!

If you want more: Rouvy

Looking for something more immersive than YouTube? I've had a Rouvy subscription for a year, and it has been a lot of fun riding in BC and all around the world.

Rouvy plays video of a real road, filmed from a bike or car, and the video moves as fast as you pedal. With a smart trainer, the pedalling resistance gets harder on the climbs and easier on the way down. You can choose whether to see yourself on the road as a little avatar, and whether to see other riders too. You don't have to race anyone. Go your own pace.

Coasting downhill in Yoho National Park, with speed, grade, and the hills ahead along the bottom · Screenshot: Rouvy

The ride settings, where you choose how many ghost riders you see and whether to hide other users · Screenshot: Rouvy

If you've heard of Zwift, it's kind of like that, but this is the real world instead of the animated fictional world in Zwift. Also Zwift is known for racing, but Rouvy is really just for riding around at your own pace. (Zwift actually bought Rouvy in April 2026, but the two still run separately.)

Rouvy runs on laptops, phones, and Apple TV. I run it on my laptop and it works great.

What you need

Unlike YouTube, Rouvy isn't just a video, so for it to "play" it has to get a signal from your bike. This means your indoor bike needs either a "smart" trainer or a speed sensor, not just the spin bike at the gym unfortunately. Here are options that work:

  • A basic indoor bike trainer and an additional speed sensor, about $200 if you find the trainer used or on sale. A basic trainer is the simple stand your back wheel sits in, with no electronics. You may already have one in the basement. Rouvy calls it a "classic trainer." Add a Bluetooth speed sensor, like Garmin's Speed Sensor 2 ($55 to $100), and Rouvy can follow along, but the hills won't get harder. Bought new at full price, a basic trainer and a sensor cost about as much as a smart trainer.
  • A smart trainer your back wheel sits in, $400 to $600 new, like the Garmin Tacx Flow Smart or Wahoo KICKR SNAP. The resistance you feel changes based on the Rouvy route. These are getting hard to find new.
  • A smart trainer you mount your bike on, with the back wheel off, $800 to $900, like the Wahoo KICKR CORE 2 or JetBlack Victory. You need a cassette that matches your bike's gears, or the single-cog version that does virtual shifting. I have an older version of this that I use with my 25-year-old "inside" bike.
  • An indoor smart bike, $2,100 to $3,800, like the Zwift Ride or Wahoo KICKR BIKE SHIFT. It's the full bike setup, so you don't attach a bike to these.

Long hours on a trainer can make your bum more sore than the same hours on the road. A good saddle helps, and so does a spongy mat or tennis balls under the trainer's feet.

What Rouvy costs

There's a 7-day free trial. After that:

  • Single: $24.99 CAD a month or $224.99 a year.
  • Duo: $39.99 a month or $349.99 a year. You invite the second person with a link, and they don't have to live with you.
  • Even larger discounts with a bigger group, or after having a membership for more than a year.

If you cancel, you keep a free version with 20 km a month. You can pause your subscription for up to 180 days once a year, which is handy if you ride outside all summer. See the prices on Rouvy's pricing page, and check out places like Reddit's r/rouvy for groups that form for discounts.

The BC rides on Rouvy

So how many of Rouvy's rides are in BC? 45! Together they cover 1,005 km of road and trail. Rouvy filmed 26 of them, and riders contributed the other 19 as community rides.

Blue rides were filmed by Rouvy and orange ones by riders. Open our map to zoom in and click each ride.

A few to look for:

  • Along the Skeena River near Prince Rupert (36 km, flat), the most-ridden BC route on Rouvy.
  • Through Pacific Rim National Park Reserve to Tofino (24 km), new in August.
  • The Stanley Park loop (9 km).
  • From Stanley Park over the Lions Gate Bridge to Howe Sound (21 km).
  • Up to Rogers Pass on Highway 1 (36 km, 756 m up).
  • The Okanagan Rail Trail from Coldstream to Lake Country (26 km, gravel), filmed by a rider.

Along the Skeena River on Highway 16 · Screenshot: Rouvy

The full list of all 45, with links and a map, is on our BC rides on Rouvy page. Rouvy's own rides have public pages. The rider-filmed ones need a Rouvy account to open.

Want to say hi? If you're on Rouvy, add me as a friend. I'm @heather_bccycletourism. I've also started a community there for riding BC routes, called Bike Beautiful British Columbia. Come join us, maybe we'll set up some group rides?

What Rouvy leaves out

Every one of Rouvy's official BC rides is on a paved road, and most are highways. Through Pacific Rim, for example, Rouvy filmed the highway, not ʔapsčiik t̓ašii (pronounced ups-cheek ta-shee), the paved path beside it which is a bit of a miss. Also, there's nothing at all in the Cariboo, the Fraser Valley, the Sunshine Coast, or the Gulf Islands yet.

Riders have filled some of the gaps. Two rail trails on Rouvy, Myra Canyon and the Okanagan Rail Trail, were both filmed by a rider this spring.

The Okanagan Rail Trail, filmed by a rider · Screenshot: Rouvy

If you have a GoPro with GPS, you can film and upload a route yourself for others to ride.

Finally, another miss: if you ride "Ride to Emerald Lake," it ends just before you get to see the lake!?!? I guess for some rewards we have to do the rides for real :)

My husband and me on the ride from Golden to Lake Louise, which goes through Yoho National Park.

PS I often listen to music, podcasts, or audiobooks (free from the library via the Libby app) while looking at the pretty virtual scenery... if you've got biking-related recommendations for those, drop me a line :)

Nov

Winter closures and detours

Kettle Valley Rail Trail: closed from Trout Crossing to Summerland after the Bald Range wildfire, likely for two to three years. Trout Crossing is where the trail meets Princeton-Summerland Road, about 13 km up the road. No detour has been posted.
Galloping Goose, Victoria: closed between Viaduct Park and Dupplin Road until spring 2027 for BC Hydro cable work. Detours are signed. The Neighbourhood Bikeway is also detoured between Gorge Road East and Culduthel Road, along Washington and Whittier avenues.
George Massey Tunnel shuttle: weekdays only until April 1. Southbound from Richmond at 6, 7, and 8 am and 4, 5, and 6 pm; northbound from Delta half an hour later. Kettle Valley closure → Galloping Goose detours → Massey shuttle schedule →

Parks Canada camping opens in January

Reservations for BC's national park campgrounds open at 8 am on these dates:
Jan 13: Glacier, Mount Revelstoke, and Gulf Islands
Jan 15: Pacific Rim (Green Point)
Jan 19: Kootenay and Yoho (8 am mountain time, which is 7 am in Vancouver)
Every campground and time is on Parks Canada's launch dates page. Missed your date? Check back, since sites open up all season.
BC's provincial parks work differently: campsites open for booking three months ahead of your arrival date, at 7 am every day. Launch dates →

Inside the Society

What the BC Cycle Tourism Society has been up to, for readers who like to see behind the scenes.

Help shape a BC bikepacking guide

We're applying to the Outdoor Recreation Council of BC for funding to make a bikepacking guide for BC. It won't cover packing lists, since others already do that well. It will cover what's specific to riding here: whose territory you're on, logging roads and gates, where you can legally wild camp in BC, and how to keep food away from wildlife in our environment(s). The first version will likely focus on central Vancouver Island. What would you want it to cover? Tell me here.

Talking cycle tourism in Victoria

Colleen MacDonald of Let's Go Biking and I spoke at the BC Cycling Coalition (BCCC) Active Transportation Summit about taking BC cycle tourism from good to great. We met people from around the province who work on active transportation in clubs, nonprofits, companies, and local and provincial governments. Thanks BCCC and all the sponsors!

Working with tourism regions for 2027

This fall we've been offering cycle tourism help to local tourism organizations around BC. That starts with a review of what they already offer riders and what's missing. Several have signed on for 2027!
