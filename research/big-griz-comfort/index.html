What you're looking at#

The Big Griz, BT 700 Bikepacking's 2,443 km bikepacking route from Victoria to Banff, matched to OpenStreetMap and coloured by our ride comfort class, the same traffic-and-surface ladder as the comfort between communities map. Ride comfort is the worse of two things: traffic, on the character map's C1 to C6 ladder, and surface, on its R0 paved to R4 extreme scale. Switch to Traffic or Surface to see each one alone, or to Grade for the climbing, from flat to brutal.

From Victoria it crosses Vancouver Island to Port Alberni, ferries over to the Sunshine Coast, and climbs the Sea to Sky to Pemberton. Then it heads through Lillooet and Merritt to the Kettle Valley Rail Trail, over the Monashees to the Arrow Lakes, across the Kootenays, and up the Elk Valley into Alberta. From Horseshoe Bay on, it follows the BC part of the Great Northern Bikepacking Route almost all the way.

What the shares say#

Over 2,246 km rated in BC, 41% is CR1 or CR2 and 25% is CR4 to CR6, roads with fast traffic. Much of the rest is gravel road and rail trail, and a good share is backcountry track. Click a stretch in the list to zoom to it.

The limits#

  • Nobody has ridden the route to check the classes. They are computed from OpenStreetMap, and the character map page explains their limits.
  • We used BT 700's route as published on Ride with GPS. The last 109 km, in Alberta, are left off: our comfort classes cover BC only.
  • Stretches run between towns the route passes. In the backcountry they are long, because there is nothing in between.
  • Where the route runs on off-road trail that the Trans Canada Trail's map marks Temporarily Closed, it glows yellow. That is only 1.1 km. The route mostly rides around the closed Kettle Valley Rail Trail.
  • Ferries are drawn blue and dashed and are not counted.
  • Matching a track to the map occasionally snaps a stretch onto a parallel road. Where a section is mapped as a footpath we matched it on foot, and it shows as not rated.
  • In the grade view, bridges and tunnels are grey because our elevation data has no deck height there.

If a stretch you know well looks wrong, we would genuinely like to hear about it: heather@bccycletourism.ca.

Methods and licence#

The traffic comfort classes and the grade scale are the character map's, and its methodology report, How we read the terrain (PDF), explains them. Ride comfort lifts a piece to CR3 where OpenStreetMap says the surface is chunky and CR4 where it is rugged, the same rule as the comfort between communities map. Backcountry is drawn magenta, as on the Trans Canada Trail page.
