What you're looking at#

Bike Across Canada is Sam Vekemans' volunteer proposal for a national cycling route network: three cross-country routes, C1 from Victoria to Cape Spear, C2 from Tofino to Halifax, and C3 from Victoria to Newfoundland by the northern way, plus the connectors, local links, access spurs, and ferries that join them. We took the British Columbia part of every track, matched it to OpenStreetMap, and coloured every piece by our ride comfort class, the same traffic-and-surface ladder as the comfort between communities map. Switch to grade to see the climbing instead, on the character map's scale from flat to brutal.

One thing to keep straight: Bike Across Canada names its routes C1, C2, and C3. Our comfort classes are CR1 to CR6. The two sets of names have nothing to do with each other.

Most of each cross-country route in BC is CR6, which on our ladder means an undivided highway shoulder: the Trans-Canada, Highway 3, Highway 97, Highway 16. That is less a verdict on these routes than on the province. Between most BC towns the highway is the only maintained road, and the comfort between communities map finds the same thing starting from the towns rather than the routes. The local connectors (CL) and access spurs (CA) sit mostly at CR1 to CR3 because they are in town, on paths and quiet streets.

Hover any line for the day-segment it belongs to and that day's full breakdown. Ferry kilometres are reported but not counted in the shares.

The limits#

  • Nobody has ridden these routes to check the classes. They are computed from OpenStreetMap, and the character map page explains their limits.
  • The routes are Bike Across Canada's proposals, not signed or maintained routes. We used the alignments published on 2026-10-05; Sam revises them, so check his site for the current version.
  • Matching a track to the map occasionally snaps a stretch onto a parallel road. The popup names the OpenStreetMap way so a miss can be spotted. Pieces that would not match at all are drawn grey.
  • In the grade view, bridges and tunnels are grey because our elevation data has no deck height there. Ferries are grey in both views.
  • The CN connectors and CW ferries include a few shuttles and bus links. They are drawn as Bike Across Canada drew them.

If a stretch you know well looks wrong, we would genuinely like to hear about it: heather@bccycletourism.ca.

Methods and licence#

The traffic comfort classes and the grade scale are the character map's, and its methodology report, How we read the terrain (PDF), explains them. Ride comfort lifts a piece to CR3 where OpenStreetMap says the surface is chunky and CR4 where it is rugged, the same rule as the comfort between communities map. On this page backcountry is drawn magenta rather than the character map's purple, because purple and CR6 are hard to tell apart on a thin line.
