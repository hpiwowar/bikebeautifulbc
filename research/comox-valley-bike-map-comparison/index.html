What you're looking at#

Every road and trail in the Comox Valley, coloured by our traffic comfort class, C1 to C6. We compute it from OpenStreetMap tags: road type, speed limit, lanes, parking, and any bike lane or path. You can see it on our own six-step scale, or grouped into three colours: C1 and C2 as green, C3 to C5 as orange, and C6 as red.

Switch to Their map to see the Comox Valley Cycling Coalition bike map (2026), traced from the published PDFs and matched road by road to OpenStreetMap. Where we differ marks each road where our colour isn't theirs. Click one for the reason, read from the tags. Their map is the answer key here. It holds local knowledge that no tag does.

The purple pins are OpenStreetMap fixes that would change our ratings most. Each opens the way in OpenStreetMap.

How often we agree#

Of the 788 km on their map that we matched to OpenStreetMap, our colour is the same as theirs on 67% of the kilometres we could compare. We rate 107 km calmer than they do and 141 km more cautious. Another 34 km isn't in our ratings at all, mostly footways with no bicycle tag.

The limits#

  • Our ratings are from OpenStreetMap as of July 2026 (OpenStreetMap extract of 2026-07-01; tags from 2026-07-05). Edits made since then, and there have been some here, aren't reflected yet. Their map was matched to OpenStreetMap as of 2026-10-04.
  • OpenStreetMap has no tag for traffic volume. We estimate it from the road type, lanes, and bus stops. That is why a painted lane on a collector road comes out C3: we assume about 4,000 vehicles a day there. On this map that is a known error in our model.
  • Where no speed limit is tagged, we assume one from the road type.
  • Only cycleway=shoulder earns credit as a bike shoulder today. A plain shoulder tag doesn't, because it says nothing about width.
  • Backcountry means a bare track or path with no bicycle or surface tag. It is off the traffic scale, not a verdict.
  • Nothing here has been checked on the ground.

Methods and licence#

The comfort classes are explained in our methodology report, How we read the terrain (PDF). The traced map is the CVCCo's own work, used with thanks, and shared with them for discussion.
