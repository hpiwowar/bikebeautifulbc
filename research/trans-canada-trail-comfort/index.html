What you're looking at#

Every cycling section of the Trans Canada Trail in British Columbia, as the trail's own map draws it, matched to OpenStreetMap and coloured by our ride comfort class, the same traffic-and-surface ladder as the comfort between communities map. Switch to grade to see the climbing instead, on the character map's scale from flat to brutal.

In BC the trail is two separate pieces. The south runs from Victoria to the Alberta border through the Lower Mainland, the Okanagan, and the Kootenays. The north follows the Alaska Highway from Dawson Creek to the Yukon. Nothing joins them inside the province.

The limits#

  • Nobody has ridden these sections to check the classes. They are computed from OpenStreetMap, and the character map page explains their limits.
  • We used the trail's cycling sections as published on tctrail.ca on 18 July 2026. Sections it marked Temporarily Closed are drawn grey and dashed and are not counted. That includes the Kettle Valley Rail Trail between Princeton and the Coldwater River, which the Province decided in February 2026 to decommission permanently.
  • Between Nelson and Balfour the official trail is a paddling route on the West Arm of Kootenay Lake. We drew the North Shore road ride to the Balfour ferry as a dashed line so the trail connects. It is ours, not the trail's, and it is not counted.
  • Ferries are drawn blue and dashed and are not counted.
  • Matching a track to the map occasionally snaps a stretch onto a parallel road. The popup names the OpenStreetMap way so a miss can be spotted. Where a section is mapped as a footpath we matched it on foot, and it shows as not rated.
  • In the grade view, bridges and tunnels are grey because our elevation data has no deck height there.

If a stretch you know well looks wrong, we would genuinely like to hear about it: heather@bccycletourism.ca.

Methods and licence#

The traffic comfort classes and the grade scale are the character map's, and its methodology report, How we read the terrain (PDF), explains them. Ride comfort lifts a piece to CR3 where OpenStreetMap says the surface is chunky and CR4 where it is rugged, the same rule as the comfort between communities map. On this page backcountry is drawn magenta rather than the character map's purple, because purple and CR6 are hard to tell apart on a thin line.
