Bike Gear Database

route network Visit Bike Gear Database ↗

Bike Gear Database is a Canadian online publication covering gravel and bikepacking gear reviews, rider stories, and community-submitted route guides. Routes are organized by country and region, including a Canada section with several British Columbia rides — and every route in the library has actually been ridden by the community before it's published.

Their BC collection

Bike Gear Database's British Columbia routes are mostly single-day gravel and mixed-surface rides of 43–124 km, spread across Vancouver Island, the Vancouver Coast and Mountains region, the Thompson-Okanagan, and the Cariboo Chilcotin Coast, plus one 510 km multi-day bikepacking route across Haida Gwaii in northern British Columbia. Surface mix ranges from fully paved to mostly unpaved (up to 83% unpaved), with difficulty from moderate to hard.

Vancouver, Coast and Mountains · 2

Photo: Hubert KangNorth Vancouver, BC
Gravel Guide Vancouver: Seymour Valley Loop
69 km · 1234 m up
AmbitiousLow-traffic ridingMixed surfaceLots of climbing

A Vancouver gravel staple: 69 km linking protected bike lanes from downtown to the Seymour Valley, where a closed-to-cars paved trailway leads deep into the forest. The route visits the Seymour Dam, source of much of Metro Vancouver's drinking water, then returns on Spur 4 Road and Fisherman's Trail past a salmon hatchery and through pockets of old growth. About 1,200 m of climbing spread gently over the day.

Photo: Alex GuiryBowen Island, BC
Texada Island Two Step
124 km · 2886 m up
ChallengingLow-traffic ridingFast-road sections · 12 kmMostly gravelChunky gravelMax climbing

A hardy 124 km lap of Texada Island, reached by ferry from Powell River. Mostly gravel, never busy, and far hillier than an island ride has any right to be: the climbing comes constantly in steep pitches, nearly 2,900 m of it. Come with low gears and a full day, or split it with a night at one of the island's campgrounds.

AmbitiousLow-traffic ridingMixed surfaceChunky gravelSome climbing

A 58 km mixed-surface loop out of Esquimalt near Victoria, about 40% unpaved with 600 m of climbing and some genuinely rough stretches. A local's line through the South Island's backroads, named for the rider who shared it.

Gordon River & Tall Trees
123 km · 3217 m up
ChallengingShares roads with trafficFast-road sections · 59 kmMixed surfaceMax climbingRemote stretches

From Lake Cowichan, this 123 km mixed-surface ride crosses to Port Renfrew and back through some of the island's most storied country: ancient forest, crystal-clear rivers, and long quiet logging-road stretches. About 3,200 m of climbing and remote in the middle, so carry what you need. The tall trees are not a figure of speech.

Tutti Loves Poutine
50 km · 205 m up

A mostly flat 50 km gravel loop built for cruising, strung with lakes and quiet forest roads. Halfway around, a roadside restaurant serves authentic Quebec-style poutine, and the Chasm viewpoint is worth the photo stop. Almost all gravel, barely any climbing, and long stretches with nobody around.

Northern British Columbia · 1

Photo: Shayd JohnsonSkidegate, BC
Haida Gwaii: Magic, Mystery and Mayhem
510 km · 5573 m up
ChallengingShares roads with trafficFast-road sections · 235 kmMixed surfaceSome climbingRemote stretches

A 510 km multi-day route through Haida Gwaii, the remote archipelago off BC's north coast. The riding threads temperate rainforest and rugged coastline, passing near ancient Haida village sites, with about 5,500 m of climbing along the way. Roughly a third is unpaved, and services are few and far between. The published account of riding it solo is a story of connection as much as cycling: this is a place you visit on its terms.
