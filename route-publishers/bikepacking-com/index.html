BIKEPACKING.com

bikepacking platform visit →

bikepacking.com is an independently operated, member-supported bikepacking media platform founded in 2012 by Logan Watts. It publishes original and curated bikepacking routes, gear reviews, adventure stories, and planning guides, with a global route network spanning nearly 50 countries, alongside a biannual print magazine, The Bikepacking Journal.

Their BC collection

bikepacking.com's British Columbia routes run from sub-100 km overnighters to multi-week expeditions such as the 1,164 km BC Trail and the roughly 1,046 km Tree to Sea Loop around northern Vancouver Island, with coverage across BC. Its route pages are unusually deep: full logistics (access, resupply, camping, difficulty) and absolutely beautiful photography. It has also singled out the West Kootenay as a destination-level bikepacking region, with a dedicated route network guide centred on Nelson (bikepacking.com/west-kootenay) linking the Kootenay Confluence, Santa Rosa Valleys and Vistas, Bonnington Scrambler, and Frog Peak Loop routes. Terrain is predominantly unpaved gravel and forest service road.

Photo: BIKEPACKING.com10 Lakes Overnighter, Powell River
10 Lakes Overnighter, Powell River
103 km · 2357 m up
ChallengingLow-traffic ridingAlmost all gravelMax climbing

The name says it: this 103 km loop from downtown Powell River strings together ten freshwater lakes on the Sunshine Coast's vast logging-road network, loosely following the famous Powell Forest Canoe Route. Fast-rolling gravel with a handful of steep climbs, and lakeside camping at spots like Nanton Lake and Fiddlehead make it an easy first overnighter. These are active, radio-controlled logging roads, so stay alert for trucks and give them the road.

Bikepacking the Sea to Sky Trail
101 km · 1917 m up
ChallengingSome traffic sharingFast-road sections · 14 kmMixed surfaceLots of singletrackLots of climbing

The Sea to Sky Trail runs 101 km from Squamish through Whistler to Pemberton, tracing a corridor the Coast Salish and Interior Salish peoples travelled long before highways. Nearly half is flowy singletrack, and the rest mixes doubletrack, gravel, and pavement past turquoise lakes, swimming holes, suspension bridges, and even an old train wreck. Climbs come short and punchy rather than long. Resupply is easy in all three towns, and public transit can carry you back to the start. Guide by Miles Arbour and Nathan Reimer.

Lower Sunshine Coast Bikepacking Route
101 km · 3107 m up
EpicLow-traffic ridingAlmost all gravelLots of singletrackChunky gravelMax climbing

A forty-minute ferry from Vancouver drops you at Earls Cove, where this route dives into a green tunnel of loamy mountain bike trails made famous by the BC Bike Race, wooden stunts and all. Three-quarters of the 101 km is singletrack, with around 3,100 m of climbing, so this is proper technical mountain biking, not a gravel ride. Sechelt sits at halfway for resupply, coffee, and brunch, and swims at Chapman Creek and Crowston Lake break up the effort. A brilliant first bikepacking trip for confident mountain bikers. Updated by Skyler Des Roches with Ian Hall and the Coast Mountain Bike Trail Association.

Powell River Sampler
84 km · 2008 m up
ChallengingLow-traffic ridingAlmost all gravelLots of singletrackChunky gravelMax climbing

The Powell River Sampler runs one way from Saltery Bay to Powell River, mixing over 50 km of loamy, ribboned singletrack with easy gravel, past waterfalls, lakes, ocean views, and the bird-rich Duck Lake protected area. Community-built cabins and lakeside campsites make the nights comfortable, and an optional hike-a-bike detour climbs to Walt Hill's 360-degree views. Rooty, technical trails call for real mountain bike skills. It links the Lower Sunshine Coast route with the Comox ferry, so the whole coast chains together.

Texada Ridge Runner Route
144 km · 2674 m up
ChallengingLow-traffic ridingFast-road sections · 12 kmMostly gravelLots of climbing

The Texada Ridge Runner traverses the island's spine from Blubber Bay to Anderson Bay and back, 144 km of quiet gravel past old farmsteads, forgotten airstrips, and beaches, with views across to Vancouver Island and the mainland mountains. The climbing is staggering for an island ride, steep and frequent, so two days is a challenge and three is a holiday. Camp oceanside at Anderson Bay or by Bob's Lake, and enjoy a rare bonus: Texada has no bears. Ferry from Powell River.

Photo: BIKEPACKING.comAlberni Bam Bam, Vancouver Island
Alberni Bam Bam, Vancouver Island
507 km · 9765 m up
AmbitiousSome traffic sharingFast-road sections · 62 kmMostly gravelLots of climbingRemote stretches

A week-long, roughly 500 km loop from Nanaimo around the south island's wild interior: gravel and rough doubletrack through the Cowichan Valley to Port Renfrew, past the Walbran Valley and Fairy Creek rainforests at the heart of BC's old-growth story, then to Bamfield, where the MV Frances Barkley, a 65-year-old heritage ship, carries you four hours up the Alberni Inlet. Swim spots and quirky communities all the way; long stretches without resupply, so plan carefully. Designed to be ridden car-free from the Departure Bay ferry.

A 190 km figure-eight starting and ending at the parliament buildings in downtown Victoria, following the Galloping Goose, Sooke Wilderness Trail, Cowichan Valley Trail, and Lochside Trail through farm and wine country. Englishman River Overnighter
52 km · 496 m up
AmbitiousLow-traffic ridingMixed surfaceLots of singletrackSome climbing

The gentlest introduction to island bikepacking: 52 km out and back from Parksville's beachfront to Englishman River Falls Provincial Park, through mossy rainforest and estuary on multi-use paths and rooty singletrack. Top Bridge's suspension bridge and swimming holes make the perfect halfway stop, and twin waterfalls wait at camp. Logistics are one online campground booking. Front suspension helps on the rootier stretches, but the route forgives.

Jeune Landing Loop
134 km · 2128 m up
AmbitiousLow-traffic ridingAlmost all gravelLots of climbing

A 134 km figure-eight linking Port McNeill and Port Alice on the North Island's rolling gravel, one of the island's most approachable bikepacking routes: no singletrack, gentle climbs, and lakeside rec-site camping all along. The country is karst, and the route passes the Devil's Bath, one of Canada's largest cenotes, and the Eternal Fountain's disappearing waterfall. A cafe in Port Alice marks halfway. The local campground reckons bikepackers are now a fifth of its guests; this is why.

A 75 km overnighter from Campbell River built around the Snowden Demonstration Forest's 100 km of trails: over 40% of the route is singletrack, flowy and sometimes rocky, linked by scenic gravel with lakeside rec-site camping to choose from. The South Island Sundowner
139 km · 1317 m up
ChallengingLow-traffic ridingMostly gravelSome climbing

A 139 km loop that never strays far from Victoria yet delivers a real taste of the island's ruggedness: rail trail and fire roads link steep, loose climbs through Thetis Lake and Francis King parks, remote singletrack in the Sooke Hills, and wide views over the Strait of Juan de Fuca to the Olympic Mountains. The numbers look tame, but a couple of kilometres of rocky hike-a-bike and relentless 20% pitches earn its reputation. Cafes and swimming lakes reward a three-day pace; strong riders chase the sundown in two.

Tree to Sea Loop, Vancouver Island
1046 km · 16742 m up
EpicSome traffic sharingFast-road sections · 92 kmMostly gravelLots of climbingRemote stretches

The North Island's bucket-list epic: a roughly 1,046 km gravel loop from Comox linking massive valleys, deep fjords, and coastal villages, Gold River, Tahsis, Zeballos, Port Alice, Holberg and its famously remote pub, on a patchwork of forest service roads. Along the way: whale watching, caves, and some of BC's best waterside camping. Plan on 15 days, a water-taxi leg from Tahsis to Zeballos, unreliable village resupply, and weather that can turn any season. Note the 2026 Strathcona Dam closure reroutes the early kilometres around Campbell Lake.

Thompson Okanagan · 4

Photo: BIKEPACKING.comBC Grasslands Circuit
BC Grasslands Circuit
250 km · 4054 m up
AmbitiousSome traffic sharingFast-road sections · 46 kmMostly gravelLots of climbing

A three-day, 250 km loop through the fragile grassland ecosystems south of Kamloops: just one percent of BC's land base, yet home to nearly a third of its threatened species. The route rolls past sprawling historic ranches, including Canada's largest at Douglas Lake, and azure lakes teeming with waterfowl; bring binoculars, this is some of the best birdwatching in the province. Good gravel and quiet pavement, but the climbs are real, around 4,000 m in all, and water is scarce, so carry plenty.

Chute Lake Charcuterie Route, Kelowna
103 km · 1690 m up
AmbitiousLow-traffic ridingMostly gravelRough, near-MTBLots of climbing

Kelowna's introduction to bikepacking: climb out of the city on the Mission Creek Greenway and Myra-Bellevue's singletrack to the Kettle Valley Rail Trail, spend the night at Chute Lake, in the lodge's glamping tents or the free rec site next door, then descend flowy trails back to City Park. The KVR stretch runs sandy and potholed, so a mountain bike is happiest. Beaches, restaurants, and breweries wait downtown when you roll back in.

Lac du Bois Tranquility, Kamloops
229 km · 4207 m up
ChallengingLow-traffic ridingFast-road sections · 14 kmMostly gravelChunky gravelLots of climbingRemote stretches

North of Kamloops lies a maze of scenic gravel through grasslands, mossy forest, and deep canyons that could be straight out of a Western. This 229 km loop through the Lac du Bois Grasslands climbs to Vidette Lake and back over three to four days, past forgotten homesteads, the Castle Rock Hoodoos, and country thick with stories, from Sasquatch sightings at Red Lake to gold panning on the Tranquille River. Long steep climbs, exposed heat, and no resupply, so carry water and take your time. Lakeside rec sites make camping easy.

Photo: BIKEPACKING.comThe BC Trail
The BC Trail
1164 km · 16531 m up
EpicLow-traffic ridingFast-road sections · 134 kmMostly gravelChunky gravelLots of climbing

The flagship BC traverse: about 1,164 km from Cultus Lake to Fernie following the province's spine of decommissioned rail trails, the Coquihalla River Trail, the KVR with its Myra Canyon trestles, and the Great Northern, linked by forest roads and occasional rocky singletrack. Rail grade does not mean easy: many sections run rough, sandy, and washboarded, hard on gear and riders alike. Plan on about 12 days, with good town amenities between the remote stretches. The BC Epic 1000 race follows it from Merritt to Fernie every June. Note: the Hope to Princeton rail trail closure currently reroutes that section via Tulameen. Skyler Des Roches linked the route together; Lennard Pretorius developed the BC Epic 1000 race that follows it.

8 Rivers Overnighter
206 km · 3251 m up
ChallengingSome traffic sharingAlmost all gravelChunky gravelLots of climbingRemote stretches

Tucked into the East Kootenays in Ktunaxa and Secwepemc territory, this 206 km loop links one spectacular river valley after another on wide gravel roads and sneaky little connectors, with a dip into Nipika's singletrack partway. The last big pass is the unanimous highlight: wildflowers, meadows, and Rockies views both directions, then hours through the staggering regrowth of old wildfires. Two to three days with creek crossings, and rustic Lussier Hot Springs waits near the start for afterwards.

Bonnington Scrambler
126 km · 3396 m up
ChallengingLow-traffic ridingMostly gravelMax climbing

A weekend loop out of Nelson for riders who like earning it: Kootenay River trails, an eclectic mix of doubletrack and gravel over the Bonnington Range, then the Great Northern Rail Trail home through the Salmo valley. Expect genuine hike-a-bike, some bushwhacking, and serious climbing for the distance. Gold-rush mining claims once blanketed these hills; today it is quiet backcountry where meeting another bikepacker is unusual. Type two fun, well worth the effort.

Frog Peak Loop
107 km · 1787 m up
AmbitiousSome traffic sharingMostly gravelLots of climbing

If you are going to convert a first-time bikepacker, this is the route: 107 km linking rail grade, scenic gravel, and sub-alpine lakes through the Slocan Valley, with the beloved Frog Peak Cafe on course and swimming holes for hot afternoons. One real climb, easy resupply, and campgrounds spaced for a relaxed weekend. The route's namesake peak carries deep significance for the Sinixt, whose Frog Mountain story tells of survival and resilience in this valley.

Photo: BIKEPACKING.comKootenay Confluence (BC Only)
Kootenay Confluence (BC Only)
473 km · 7370 m up
AmbitiousLow-traffic ridingFast-road sections · 57 kmMostly gravelLots of climbingRemote stretches

The flagship of the West Kootenay bikepacking network: a nine-day, 600 km loop from Nelson through the Slocan Valley, the Arrow Lakes, and Grand Forks, crossing into Washington and returning along the Pend d'Oreille, with about 470 km on the BC side. The riding sticks to rail grades, gravel roads, and pavement, with lakes and rivers to cool off in nearly the whole way. The route crosses boundaries on purpose, telling this region's story from once-booming mining towns to the Sinixt, declared extinct by Canada in 1956 and recognized again by the Supreme Court in 2021.

Log Jam Loop
101 km · 1577 m up
ChallengingLow-traffic ridingAlmost all gravelChunky gravelLots of climbingRemote stretches

An accessible overnighter in the valleys around Nakusp and Slocan Lake: 101 km of smooth gravel, bumpy quad track, and rail grade that blurs into singletrack. Detours tempt the whole way, from Wilson Creek Falls dropping 63 m into a pool worth the hike, to New Denver's cafes and shops, to Slocan Lake's clear water at the finish. Santa Rosa Valleys and Vistas

ChallengingSome traffic sharingMostly gravelChunky gravelMax climbing

Cariboo Chilcotin Coast · 3

Photo: BIKEPACKING.comBest of the South Chilcotin Mountains
Best of the South Chilcotin Mountains
121 km · 3393 m up
EpicLow-traffic ridingAlmost all gravelLots of singletrackRough, near-MTBMax climbingRemote stretches

Canada's bikepacking hotspot, ridden in a long weekend: a 121 km loop linking the South Chilcotins' most rideable singletrack through mid-elevation meadows, dry forest, and open alpine under high peaks. It is all unpaved, mostly trail, and genuinely remote, with no resupply or cell service, so arrive self-sufficient. Strong fitness and mountain bike skills turn it from an ordeal into the highlight of your year. Wildflowers peak in June and July.

Bikepacking the Camelsfoot Range
158 km · 4720 m up
EpicLow-traffic ridingAlmost all gravelLots of singletrackRough, near-MTBMax climbingRemote stretches

If variety is the spice of life, this is a five-star route: dreamy doubletrack, rowdy descents, open alpine freeriding along Nine Mile Ridge, and one grueling push to earn it. The 158 km loop near Lillooet is remote even by BC standards, with no real bail options, so pack light and be ready to fix anything. Even the fittest riders manage about 40 km a day. Marmots, bighorn sheep, and grizzlies live here.

Coast Mountains Bikepacking
772 km · 16762 m up
EpicLow-traffic ridingFast-road sections · 41 kmAlmost all gravelChunky gravelMax climbingRemote stretches

The expedition: 772 km along the dry eastern edge of the Coast Mountains, from Harrison Lake to Tatla Lake, through canyons, alpine tundra, and country where a broken bike can mean a three-day walk. Between the wild stretches come cultural ones, salmon drying in the wind along the Fraser at Lillooet, and the Xeni Gwet'in ranch country of the Nemiah Valley. Plan on two weeks, around 16,000 m of climbing, and pre-arranged food drops.

Northern British Columbia · 1

Photo: BIKEPACKING.comAround the Babines
Around the Babines
110 km · 2085 m up
AmbitiousLow-traffic ridingFast-road sections · 13 kmMostly gravelLots of climbingRemote stretches

A 110 km loop from Smithers that packs in the Babine Mountains' best: 60 km of scenic rolling gravel, then a purpose-built singletrack climb through conifer forest to the old Cronin fire lookout, over Hyland Pass past the abandoned Silver King Mine, and into a 23 km descent to the Joe L'Orsa Cabin, a beautiful spot to spend the night. Steep climbs, possible snow early in the season, and no resupply, so carry everything for two to three days. Back in Smithers in time for brunch.
