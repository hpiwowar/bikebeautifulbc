BT 700 Bikepacking

individual route creator visit →

BT 700 Bikepacking is a route-publishing site run by Ontario adventure cyclist and writer Matt Kadey, one of Canada's most prolific bikepacking route builders. It grew out of the original BT700 gravel loop in southwestern Ontario and now offers downloadable route files for gravel and bikepacking rides across Canada, including several in British Columbia.

Their BC collection

BT 700 Bikepacking's British Columbia routes are side trips, bypasses, and connectors along The Big Griz, its 2,380 km Victoria-to-Banff flagship — from 37 km connectors up to a 304 km multi-day route into Castlegar. Six are concentrated in the Kootenay-Rockies region (Fernie, Nakusp, New Denver, and the Central and East Kootenay), two are on Vancouver Island (Bamfield, Port Renfrew), and one extends into the Thompson-Okanagan. Most are unpaved or mixed-surface gravel routes with significant climbing, rated moderate to hard.

BT700 Bamfield
110 km · 972 m up
AmbitiousSome traffic sharingFast-road sections · 9 kmAlmost all gravelSome climbingRemote stretches

A 110 km mostly-gravel leg into Bamfield, the remote boardwalk village on Barkley Sound reached otherwise by boat or the same long logging roads you will ride. Steady climbing, rough stretches, and long quiet distances; the reward is one of the west coast's most distinctive destinations. Route created by Matt Kadey of BT 700 Bikepacking.

BT700 Port Renfrew
174 km · 3731 m up
ChallengingShares roads with trafficFast-road sections · 51 kmMixed surfaceMax climbingRemote stretches

A 174 km BT700 leg from the Victoria area out to Port Renfrew on the island's wild west coast, on a near-even mix of pavement and gravel with a serious 3,700 m of climbing. Remote in the middle third and shared with traffic on the road sections. Big trees and the Pacific wait at the end. Route created by Matt Kadey of BT 700 Bikepacking.

BT700 Castlegar
304 km · 3490 m up
AmbitiousLow-traffic ridingFast-road sections · 13 kmMostly gravelChunky gravelSome climbingRemote stretches

A 304 km multi-day BT700 stretch running down to Castlegar, about three-quarters unpaved with 3,500 m of climbing spread over gentle grades. The surfaces are the challenge here rather than the hills: expect long rough stretches where fatter tires and patience both pay off. Route created by Matt Kadey of BT 700 Bikepacking.

The BT700's connector between Fernie and Sparwood through the Elk Valley: 37 km, about three-quarters unpaved, with some genuinely chunky surfaces and around 570 m of climbing. A solid half-day link between the two towns. Route created by Matt Kadey of BT 700 Bikepacking.

BT700 Gray Creek Bypass
174 km · 1991 m up
AmbitiousShares roads with trafficMostly fast roadsMostly pavedSome climbing

The BT700's paved alternative through the Central Kootenays via Gray Creek: 174 km with about 2,000 m of rolling climbing, nearly all on pavement and highway, so expect to ride with traffic. The practical choice when your bike or the conditions make the rougher passes a bad idea. Route created by Matt Kadey of BT 700 Bikepacking.

BT700 Kaslo
85 km · 2103 m up
ChallengingSome traffic sharingFast-road sections · 30 kmMostly gravelMax climbing

An 85 km BT700 leg with one of BC's more memorable trail moments: a hand-pulled cable-car river crossing on the Galena Trail out of New Denver. From there the Wagon Trail runs rough and chunky into Kaslo, then paved highway follows Kootenay Lake south to Balfour, with about 2,100 m of climbing along the way. Route created by Matt Kadey of BT 700 Bikepacking.
