Cross Canada Cycle Tour Society

touring organization visit →

The Cross Canada Cycle Tour Society (CCCTS) is a non-profit cycling club founded in 1982, based in British Columbia with a chapter in Ottawa. In its own words, it is a club for active adults of all ages; members are mainly retired people who love recreational cycling. Volunteers organize weekly day rides plus multi-day and cross-Canada tours, some of which have run continuously since the 1980s.

Their BC collection

The Cross Canada Cycle Tour Society's BC routes range from short day rides of about 84 km to a 1,511 km cross-provincial tour toward Calgary. Most of its routes here are on Vancouver Island, with others in the Lower Mainland and Fraser Valley and one crossing northern British Columbia, the Cariboo-Chilcotin Coast, and the Thompson-Okanagan. Most routes are on paved roads with rolling-to-climbing terrain, and the set includes several multi-day touring routes and one route linked by four ferry crossings.

Bakerview to Fort Langley
84 km · 490 m up
AmbitiousShares roads with trafficMostly pavedGentle grades

A rolling 84 km paved day ride through the Fraser Valley between Bakerview and Fort Langley, with about 490 m of climbing. The destination earns the miles: Fort Langley's national historic site and cafe-lined village make a natural turnaround. Club-style road riding, so expect to share the road.

Cross Canada - Vancouver to Hope
176 km · 1270 m up
ModerateShares roads with trafficFast-road sections · 36 kmMostly pavedSome climbing

Every cross-Canada tour has a first day, and this is the Cross Canada Cycle Tour Society's: 176 km from Vancouver to Hope through the Fraser Valley, nearly flat until the mountains gather at the valley's end. About 1,270 m of climbing, almost all paved, and mostly on roads you will share with traffic. From Hope, the northern and southern cross-country routes part ways.

The fifth day of a Cross Canada Cycle Tour Society multi-day tour, riding 110 km in the Nanaimo area with about 1,100 m of rolling climbing, nearly all paved. Club-style road touring: steady pace, shared roads, coffee halfway.

Photo: Danika McDowellComox, BC
4 Ferry Odyssey
165 km · 1662 m up
AmbitiousShares roads with trafficMostly fast roadsMostly pavedSome climbing

The 4 Ferry Odyssey links Campbell River, Quadra Island, Cortes Island, Lund, and Powell River in a 165 km island-hopping tour with four water crossings, three ferries and a water taxi. The riding is nearly all paved but deceptively hilly, and the crossings set an unforgiving rhythm, which is why the club recommends it for strong riders. Few routes pack in more Salish Sea per kilometre.

Cross Canada - Far North - Vancouver Island
498 km · 3461 m up
ChallengingShares roads with trafficMostly fast roadsMostly pavedSome climbingRemote stretches

The Vancouver Island stage of the Cross Canada Cycle Tour Society's Far North route: 498 km running the island's length on paved highway and back roads, with about 3,500 m of climbing. The northern half turns quiet and remote, with long gaps between services and highway traffic for company. A committed multi-day tour and a fine way to understand how big the island really is.

Duncan to Chemainus
108 km · 729 m up
AmbitiousShares roads with trafficMostly fast roadsMostly pavedSome climbing

A rolling 108 km paved day ride between Duncan, the City of Totems, and Chemainus, famous for its outdoor murals, through the Cowichan Valley's farmland. About 730 m of climbing on shared roads; the two towns' public art makes better-than-average turnaround entertainment.

Gold River 2
109 km · 1421 m up
AmbitiousShares roads with trafficMostly fast roadsMostly pavedLots of climbingRemote stretches

An out-and-back paved ride on Highway 28 from the Elk Portal parking area to Gold River and on to the salt water at Muchalat Inlet, about 109 km with 1,400 m of climbing. Quiet mountain highway riding through the edge of Strathcona country, with long empty stretches, so carry what you need.

An 84 km rolling day ride through Greater Victoria, mostly paved, sampling the capital region's shoreline drives, quiet neighbourhoods, and rural edges with only about 400 m of climbing. A club-style tour that shows how much variety hides within an afternoon of the city.

Victoria - Port Renfrew - Lake Cowichan - Duncan - Victoria
264 km · 2993 m up
AmbitiousShares roads with trafficMostly fast roadsMostly pavedSome climbing

A classic South Island road tour: start at the Empress Hotel in downtown Victoria, ride the coast road to Port Renfrew, cross the island's interior to Lake Cowichan and Duncan, and return, 264 km over two or three days with nearly 3,000 m of climbing. Paved throughout, remote and lightly serviced on the west-coast leg, and about as varied as island road riding gets.

Northern British Columbia · 1

Photo: Mattias FredrikssonHazelton, BC
Cross Canada Far North (BC Only)
1029 km · 9045 m up
EpicShares roads with trafficMostly fast roadsMostly pavedSome climbingRemote stretches

The British Columbia portion of the Cross Canada Cycle Tour Society's Far North route from Prince Rupert to Calgary: about 1,030 km of paved touring across the northern interior, through the Cariboo and the Thompson Okanagan, before crossing into Alberta. Around 9,000 m of climbing in all, with long remote stretches and highway riding throughout. A serious self-supported tour through country most cyclists never see.
