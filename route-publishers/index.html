The people behind the routes

Every ride on Get Inspired was researched, mapped, and written up by one of these 16 route publishers. We point; they know the way. Here's who they are, in full — and their showcased BC rides.

Let's Go Bikingguidebook author
179 BC rides showcased here

Let's Go Biking is a British Columbia cycling guidebook series written by Colleen MacDonald, a champion of slow, scenic, family-friendly riding. Her three guidebooks — Around Vancouver; Okanagan and Beyond; and Vancouver Island, the Gulf Islands and Sunshine Coast — come with a companion website of mapped rides, walks, and runs.

Their story + 179 BC rides →Visit Let's Go Biking ↗
BIKEPACKING.combikepacking platform
26 BC rides showcased here

bikepacking.com is an independently operated, member-supported bikepacking media platform founded in 2012 by Logan Watts. It publishes original and curated bikepacking routes, gear reviews, adventure stories, and planning guides, with a global route network spanning nearly 50 countries, alongside a biannual print magazine, The Bikepacking Journal.

Their story + 26 BC rides →Visit BIKEPACKING.com ↗
Gravel Buddiescycling club
23 BC rides showcased here

Gravel Buddies is a Vancouver-based cycling community that organizes group gravel rides for women, gender-diverse, and 2SLGBTQIA+ riders, ranging from short evening rides in Stanley Park to multi-day bikepacking trips.

Their story + 23 BC rides →Visit Gravel Buddies ↗
Love Machineindividual route creator
16 BC rides showcased here

Love Machine is a Vancouver-based gravel cycling project run by two friends, Max and Cam, as a side project — not a brand, shop, or club. It publishes free, downloadable GPX routes across southwestern British Columbia, with a stated goal of keeping gravel cycling welcoming rather than gatekept.

Their story + 16 BC rides →Visit Love Machine ↗
Trails BCtrail organization
12 BC rides showcased here

Trails BC (the Trails Society of BC) is a non-profit charity that advocates for greenway trails for self-propelled recreation and active transportation across the province. Léon Lebrun has been at the heart of that work for nearly three decades, building and championing non-motorized trails across the Lower Mainland and beyond; a bridge in Chilliwack and a trail in Port Moody now carry his name. The rides shown here are a handful of the many he's helped map over the years.

Their story + 12 BC rides →Visit Trails BC ↗
Bikepack Canadabikepacking platform
12 BC rides showcased here

Bikepack Canada is a bikepacking route-sharing platform founded in 2016 by adventure cyclist Ryan Correy, author of the guidebook 'Bikepacking in the Canadian Rockies' and the memoir 'A Purpose Ridden.' Correy died in 2018; the site continues today, hosting routes contributed by bikepackers from across the country.

Their story + 12 BC rides →Visit Bikepack Canada ↗
Bike Gear Databaseroute network
10 BC rides showcased here

Bike Gear Database is a Canadian online publication covering gravel and bikepacking gear reviews, rider stories, and community-submitted route guides. Routes are organized by country and region, including a Canada section with several British Columbia rides — and every route in the library has actually been ridden by the community before it's published.

Their story + 10 BC rides →Visit Bike Gear Database ↗
Cross Canada Cycle Tour Societytouring organization
10 BC rides showcased here

The Cross Canada Cycle Tour Society (CCCTS) is a non-profit cycling club founded in 1982, based in British Columbia with a chapter in Ottawa. In its own words, it is a club for active adults of all ages; members are mainly retired people who love recreational cycling. Volunteers organize weekly day rides plus multi-day and cross-Canada tours, some of which have run continuously since the 1980s.

Their story + 10 BC rides →Visit Cross Canada Cycle Tour Society ↗
BT 700 Bikepackingindividual route creator
10 BC rides showcased here

BT 700 Bikepacking is a route-publishing site run by Ontario adventure cyclist and writer Matt Kadey, one of Canada's most prolific bikepacking route builders. It grew out of the original BT700 gravel loop in southwestern Ontario and now offers downloadable route files for gravel and bikepacking rides across Canada, including several in British Columbia.

Their story + 10 BC rides →Visit BT 700 Bikepacking ↗
Adventure Cycling Associationtouring organization
6 BC rides showcased here

For over 50 years, Adventure Cycling Association has been mapping the most iconic bike routes in North America, including the TransAmerica Trail and the Great Divide Mountain Bike Route. It hosts over 90,000 kilometres of bikepacking and bike touring routes, leads dozens of guided tours each year, and advocates to protect the routes, access, and infrastructure that make bike travel possible. Supported by members and donors, Adventure Cycling's nonprofit mission is to inspire, empower, and connect people to travel by bike.

Their story + 6 BC rides →Visit Adventure Cycling Association ↗
Elbow Valley Cycle Clubcycling club
6 BC rides showcased here

Elbow Valley Cycle Club is a Calgary-based recreational cycling club, one of the largest in Western Canada with roughly 700 members. It organizes about 250 events a year, including road, gravel, and multi-day touring rides, several of which cross into British Columbia.

Their story + 6 BC rides →Visit Elbow Valley Cycle Club ↗
British Columbia Randonneurscycling club
3 BC rides showcased here

British Columbia Randonneurs Cycling Club is a long-distance cycling club with deep roots: randonneuring in BC dates to 1979, and the club was a founding member of Les Randonneurs Mondiaux, the world body for 1,200 km grand randonnées, in 1983. With regional chapters in the Lower Mainland, Vancouver Island, Southern Interior, and Peace Region, it organizes brevets (timed long-distance rides of 200 km and up), populaires (shorter introductory events), and permanents (self-paced routes available year-round), and publishes the routes on Ride with GPS. Its celebrated Rocky Mountain 1200 drew riders from around the world.

Their story + 3 BC rides →Visit British Columbia Randonneurs ↗
The Great Community Route Celebrationevent organizer
2 BC rides showcased here

The Great Community Route Celebration was a global cycling route-creation event hosted by Ride with GPS, running June 11 to July 5, 2020. Participants worldwide created and submitted routes highlighting places and spaces in their own communities, adding points of interest and photos along the way. The event drew 523 route submissions from 51 countries.

Their story + 2 BC rides →Visit The Great Community Route Celebration ↗
Trans Canada Trailtrail organization
2 BC rides showcased here

Trans Canada Trail is a national charitable organization, based in Ottawa, that coordinates and maintains the Trans Canada Trail (also known as The Great Trail) — a roughly 30,000 km multi-use trail network spanning every Canadian province and territory and connecting the Atlantic, Pacific, and Arctic coastlines. The network supports hiking, cycling, paddling, and other activities, and is built and managed through partnerships with regional and local trail organizations rather than owned as a single continuous trail.

Their story + 2 BC rides →Visit Trans Canada Trail ↗
Great Northern Bikepacking Routeindividual route creator
2 BC rides showcased here

The Great Northern Bikepacking Route (GNBR) is a mostly off-road bikepacking route created by Ontario adventure cyclist and writer Matt Kadey, spanning over 13,000 km across 10 Canadian provinces and 3 U.S. states, from the Pacific to the Atlantic — one of the most ambitious route-mapping projects in the country. It is published segment by segment on a dedicated website, with route data on RideWithGPS and gear and provisioning notes alongside the maps.

Their story + 2 BC rides →Visit Great Northern Bikepacking Route ↗
Trans Canada Bike Route Networkroute network
1 BC ride showcased here

A volunteer-run, community-collaborative project mapping a proposed coast-to-coast National Cycling Route Network for Canada. It publishes route data as GPX files, a Garmin BaseCamp database (GDB) with points of interest, KMZ files for offline smartphone use, interactive Google Maps, and a curated Ride with GPS collection.

Their story + 1 BC rides →Visit Trans Canada Bike Route Network ↗
