With Thanks

This site runs on generosity. Much of what you see here, the trails, the maps, the data, the hosting, exists because people and organizations chose to share their work openly, so others could build on it.

The people on the ground#

Every trail on this site exists because someone builds and maintains it, usually a volunteer trail society working with hand tools and small budgets. If you ride these trails, support the trail organizations.

The routes we feature were designed by real people who scouted, mapped, and documented them. Meet the route makers.

Riding in BC keeps getting better because bike advocacy organizations have spent decades building bike-friendly culture, laws, and infrastructure. Thank you to the BC Cycling Coalition, HUB Cycling, GoByBike BC, and the many local groups doing this work in their own communities.

OpenStreetMap#

The foundation of this site. Our maps, our directory of bike stops, and our route data all draw on OpenStreetMap, a world map built by volunteers. Thousands of mappers have surveyed British Columbia's roads, trails, campgrounds, and cafés so that anyone can use the results for free. We are grateful to every one of them.

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License.

Open data#

  • The Province of British Columbia, through the BC Data Catalogue and GeoBC: tourism region, municipality, and regional district boundaries, the BC Geographical Names register, the Freshwater Atlas, and listings for BC Parks and Recreation Sites and Trails BC.
  • Statistics Canada census data and NASA's Shuttle Radar Topography Mission elevation data, which powers our steepness data.
  • The Commission for Environmental Cooperation's North American Land Change Monitoring System (NALCMS) land cover data, which helps us guess the surface of backcountry tracks nobody has mapped, and spot supply stops that are across water.
  • BC Transit and TransLink open schedule data, and BC Ferries schedules, which power our travel planning pages.
  • Indigenous Tourism BC (ITBC), whose business listings help us identify Indigenous-owned and Indigenous-operated stops.
  • HUB Cycling, whose comfort classification work informs how we describe cycling conditions, and CAN-BICS, the Canadian Bikeway Comfort and Safety Classification system, developed by Simon Fraser University's CHATR Lab with the Public Health Agency of Canada and now used by Statistics Canada to map cycling infrastructure nationally.

Open source software#

This site is built with free, open source software. MapLibre GL and the OpenMapTiles schema render the interactive maps. PostgreSQL with the PostGIS extension and Valhalla power the cycling route network we build behind the scenes, and the Python tools osmium and Shapely do much of the data work. Thank you to the maintainers and contributors, and to the many other projects we rely on.

Generous programs#

  • GitHub Pages hosts this site for free.
  • Google Places offers a free tier that helps us check our facts, like whether a place is still open, against Google's fresher data.
  • Destination BC's Content Hub provides much of the photography on this site. Individual photographers are credited on each image.

Spotted something wrong?#

Corrections are a contribution too. If a listing is out of date, a trail is misdescribed, or a map is wrong, suggest a fix. We read every one, and we're thankful for each.
