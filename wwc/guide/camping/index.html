Camping guide for cyclists in British Columbia

Campgrounds are essential cycle tourism infrastructure for bikepackers and others. The key question is not just whether camping exists, but whether a rider arriving under their own power can realistically stay there.

This guide highlights camping options that may be useful to cyclists travelling in British Columbia, including public and private campgrounds, national, provincial, and regional parks, recreation sites, and other overnight outdoor stays. Cyclist-relevant details can include hiker-biker space, no-turn-away practices, potable water, washrooms, food access, charging, safe places to leave loaded bikes, and whether reservations are required.

Use this page to understand what makes a campground useful for bike travel, then open the interactive directory to search for camping options along your route.

What makes a camping stop welcoming for cyclists?#

Core criteria

  • No turn-away policy for cyclists

Helpful extras we track for camping stops

  • Washrooms & shower details: Showers, Washrooms, Flush toilets, Pit/outhouse toilet
  • Food & water: Potable water, Food nearby, Vending machines, Ice, Camping fuel
  • Charging: Device charging, E-bike charging
  • Gear & comfort: Covered shelter, Laundry, Drying area
  • Booking & boarding: Min-stay waived, Hiker/biker sites, Animal-safe food storage, Reservations accepted, Cyclist discount
  • Bike care: Pump, Basic tools

Other places to sleep#

Also, here are two more sources of places to camp, not included in our listings:

  • Hipcamp · campsites on private land: farms, wineries, backyards, bookable online
  • Warm Showers · a worldwide community of cyclists hosting cyclists, often a bed and sometimes a spot for your tent in the yard; free to use after a one-time US$30 signup
